# Mol3D

Type a compound name and see the molecule in 3D, with buttons for the functional groups it contains. A static site for students: no login, works on phones, tablets and laptops.

## How it works

1. **Name → structure.** The browser asks [OPSIN](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/opsin/) (EMBL-EBI) to parse the name as a systematic (IUPAC) name. If OPSIN cannot parse it, the browser asks [PubChem](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/) to look it up as a common or trade name. Both services allow direct calls from the browser (CORS), so there is no server.
2. **Structure → 3D.** [OpenChemLib](https://github.com/cheminfo/openchemlib-js) builds a conformer and relaxes it with the MMFF94s+ force field, in a web worker so the page stays responsive. For salts and mixtures only the largest part is shown. The conformer generator sometimes drops a set cis/trans (cis-1,2-dichloroethene came out trans), so a conformer whose stereo differs from the structure's is thrown away and the next seed tried.
3. **Tabs, one per concept.**
   - **Structure:** buttons for the 21 functional groups in `chem.js` (SMARTS + a short explanation). Tap an atom to see its element, hybridization (textbook rule: a lone pair next to a π bond counts as sp²), lone pairs, and each bond angle measured on the model next to the ideal 109.5°/120°/180°.
     - **Polarity:** a see-through arrow along each polar bond, δ+ → δ− (Pauling electronegativity difference 0.4 or more), and polar or nonpolar from their vector sum on the 3D model, with a gold arrow for the net direction. No Debye numbers. The cutoff is 0.4, not 0.5, because C–N (0.49) and C≡N must count (pyridine, acetonitrile, trimethylamine are polar); C–H (0.35) does not. The molecule is polar when the sum is 0.1 or more: in `test.mjs` symmetric molecules sum to under 0.02 (BF₃ 0.06, since MMFF94s+ has no boron types and the raw conformer stays) and polar ones to 0.41 or more. Lone pairs are left out, as in the textbook sketch, and the shape counts (1,2-dichloroethane: anti nonpolar, gauche polar). Ions and molecules with I, S, P or other elements outside H, B, C, N, O, F, Cl, Br get no verdict, since C–I and C–S bonds are polar in practice while their electronegativity difference says otherwise.
     - **Aromatic rings:** each ring OpenChemLib calls aromatic, with its π electrons and 4n + 2 check, and for fused rings the whole ring system's count (naphthalene and indole 10). Each atom adds 1 for a double bond (to an atom of the same ring system), 2 for a lone pair in its p orbital (pyrrole N–H, furan O, a ring C⁻), 0 for an empty p orbital (ring C⁺); a lone pair beside a ring double bond lies in the ring plane (pyridine N) and adds 0. Tap a ring to turn it edge-on and see a see-through p-orbital lobe above and below each ring atom. `test.mjs` checks that the atoms giving a lone pair are exactly the ones the hybridization rule calls sp² by resonance.
     - **Equivalent H (¹H NMR):** a letter (and one of 8 colors; the letter is the mark) on each H, with the number of signals. Two H are equivalent when swapping each for D gives the same compound or its mirror image (OpenChemLib canonical code with stereo, read from the 3D model): enantiotopic H count as one, diastereotopic H (the CH₂ of 2-chlorobutane) as two. The code ignores conformation, so CH₃ rotation and chair flips average out (cyclohexane: 1). OpenChemLib's symmetry ranks are not used: they are undefined for H atoms. O–H and N–H are counted, with a note that exchange can change them in a real spectrum.
     - **Guess first** (off by default) for polar or nonpolar and for the number of signals.
   - **Kit:** the parts list for the Darling *Molecular Visions* kit, following Dr. Dan Berger's [guide](https://homepages.bluffton.edu/~bergerd/Models/visions.html) (Bluffton University). Aromatic rings count as one Kekulé structure. Parts the guide does not cover (halogens, S, P, C≡N, …) are listed to check against the kit.
   - **Stereo:** R/S for each stereocenter, E/Z for each stereo double bond (CIP rules), cis/trans for 1,2-disubstituted C=C, and cis/trans between substituted ring atoms. When the source structure leaves one open, the page says the 3D build picked it.
     - Tap a stereocenter to see its CIP priorities (1–4) on the model, and turn the model so group 4 points away. The priorities come from `chem.js` (rules 1 and 2 on the hierarchical digraph, with duplicate atoms); a center is shown only when they give the same R/S as OpenChemLib. Pseudoasymmetric centers (r/s) and the rare centers that need rules 3–5 are left out.
     - **Guess first** (off by default) hides R/S and the priorities: pick R or S to see the answer, or tap Hint to see the priorities and turn group 4 away first.
     - **Mirror image** shows the reflected molecule beside the model, R and S swapped, and says whether the two are superimposable (enantiomers, or one meso compound).
     - **Mirror plane** (meso compounds: superimposable on the mirror image, with two or more stereocenters, pseudoasymmetric r/s centers not counted) draws the plane inside the molecule as a see-through plate and turns the view to look along it. The plane is drawn only on real coordinates that pass a check: each atom, reflected through it, lands within 0.3 Å of its partner (partners from a substitution test: mark an atom with an isotope and see which marked compound is its mirror image), one partner each, with no two atoms three or more bonds apart closer than 1.5 Å. The plane swaps two stereocenters, so it is their perpendicular bisector. When the model as built has no plane (the anti form of meso-tartaric acid has only a center of symmetry), the model is turned about single bonds, as on the Shape tab: first the bonds on the chain between the two stereocenters, together on a 5° grid, then each other bond in turn. If that fails, conformers from the generator (MMFF94s+, converged runs, same stereo) are tried the same way and moved onto the model. Ring bonds never turn, so cis-1,2-dimethylcyclohexane (both chairs are chiral) gets "This compound has a mirror plane in another conformation", with a note that for a ring this is the flat ring the textbook draws. `test.mjs` checks meso-tartaric acid, meso-2,3-dibromobutane, meso-2,4-pentanediol, meso-2,5-hexanediol, ribaric acid, cis-1,2-dimethylcyclopropane and cis-1,3-dimethylcyclohexane (plane found, each atom reflecting onto an atom of its element, bond lengths and stereo unchanged), and (2R,3R)-tartaric acid, (R)-2-butanol and cis-1,4-dimethylcyclohexane (not meso).
     - **Fischer projection:** the longest chain of carbons outside rings that holds every chain stereocenter, top to bottom, drawn as SVG with R/S beside each stereocenter. The more oxidized end (more bonds to O) goes on top; a tie goes to the end IUPAC numbers 1 (lowest locants for the principal group, then multiple bonds, then all substituents). Which horizontal group goes right comes from the 3D model's configuration: with the chain neighbors U (up) and D (down) and a group A around center c, the sign of det(U − c, D − c, A − c) decides, so any conformation gives the same drawing (`test.mjs` checks this with a second D-glucose conformer). Open-chain sugars get D/L from the lowest stereocenter and α-amino acids from the α-carbon (L-threonine is L by C2, not C3). A meso compound drawn this way reads the same from the bottom up and gets a dashed mirror line. With one stereocenter, **Turn to the Fischer view** turns the view (not the coordinates) so the vertical bonds point back. Molecules whose stereocenters are all in rings say "No Fischer projection: the stereocenters are in a ring." `test.mjs` checks the textbook layouts (D-glyceraldehyde, D- and L-glucose, D-galactose, D-fructose, L-alanine, L-serine, L-threonine, L-leucine, both tartaric acids, meso-2,3-dibromobutane, meso-2,4-pentanediol, both 2-butanols) and reads R/S off each drawing (vertical groups back, horizontal ones forward) against the R/S from the 3D model.
     - **Compare two structures:** a second name, looked up the same way and built in 3D beside the model (never together with the mirror image), and how the two are related. In order: the molecular formula (different: not isomers); the connectivity, from OpenChemLib's canonical code with stereo removed (different: constitutional isomers); then the canonical code with stereo, read from each 3D model, and the code of the second one's mirror image (same compound, enantiomers, or diastereomers). A meso compound is its own mirror image, so it comes out the same compound as itself. When the stereo of either structure is not set in the name (2-butanol), the page says the relationship cannot be told, once the connectivity matches (1-butanol and 2-butanol are constitutional isomers either way). Conformations do not count: two models that differ only by turning about single bonds are the same compound. `test.mjs` checks the textbook pairs against RDKit's canonical SMILES (the mirror image inverts every @/@@).
     - **Guess first** (off by default) for the relationship (five choices) and for the Fischer projection (D or L for sugars and amino acids, otherwise R or S at each stereocenter).
   - **Shape:** conformations and ring strain.
     - **Newman:** each C–C single bond outside a ring with two sp³ carbons (one per symmetry class). Tap one to look straight down it, front carbon toward you; a slider turns the back carbon, and the page says staggered or eclipsed, plus anti or gauche when each carbon carries exactly one non-H group.
     - **Energy curve:** schematic, order only (no numbers). It follows the textbook rule: eclipsed above staggered, and each eclipsed or gauche pair of carbon groups raises the energy. It is drawn only when every group on the two carbons is H, an sp³ carbon, or a C=C or benzene-ring carbon, since polar groups (C=O, O, N, halogens) can change the order. A C=C or benzene-ring group counts when eclipsed but not when gauche: MMFF94s+ puts its gauche form only 0.3–0.9 kJ/mol above anti (propylbenzene, 1-pentene), against 3.3 kJ/mol for butane, and `test.mjs` checks this. OpenChemLib cannot hold a dihedral fixed while it relaxes the rest (a relaxed scan), and a rigid scan overstates butane's barriers by about 2×, so the curve does not use force-field energies. **Guess first** (off by default) hides the curve until the student turns to the form they think is least stable.
     - **Steric (van der Waals) strain:** on bonds that get a curve, a see-through pink rod with a "vdW strain" label joins each pair of non-H groups sitting gauche or closer (Newman angle 65° or less; a C=C or benzene-ring group only when eclipsed), and the panel names them (gauche, eclipsed, or how far apart). Pairs with H are left out, following the textbook split: CH₃/CH₃ gauche is steric strain, H/CH₃ eclipsed is mostly torsional.
     - **Chair (axial / equatorial):** each six-membered ring of sp³ atoms that is a chair in the model (its six ring torsions alternate in sign, each at least 30°). Tap one to see it from the side, with "ax"/"eq" beside each group that is not H (axial = bond within about 50° of the ring's axis). Newman bonds, chairs and ring-angle rows deselect each other.
       - **1,3-diaxial strain:** each axial group (not H) gets a pink rod and a "1,3-diaxial" label to every axial atom two ring atoms away on the same face, and the panel names the pairs.
       - **Ring flip:** computed only when pressed, in the worker. OpenChemLib's conformer generator makes 40 conformers (one per seed), MMFF94s+ relaxes each fully (only converged runs count), and a chair with every group's axial/equatorial swapped is the flipped chair. The lowest one that keeps each group on its face is moved onto the model's ring (each ring H on its own face too: the generator does not keep which H of a CH₂ is which); no coordinates are made up. The slide between the two chairs is a straight line, and the page says the real path goes through half-chair and twist-boat forms. The generator never builds the axial chair for groups larger than CH₃ or OH (tert-butyl, ethyl, isopropyl, phenyl, menthol, glucose), so those say "flip not available". Only-H rings, bridged rings and fused rings are not flipped, with the reason shown (cis-fused rings like cis-decalin can flip, but only with the other ring, which the page does not show).
       - **More stable chair:** the lowest MMFF94s+ energy of each chair, compared: "This chair is more stable", or "Both chairs are about the same" under 1 kJ/mol. It is given only for alkyl groups, OH and NH₂, which `test.mjs` checks against measured A-values (MMFF94s+ Δ axial − equatorial: CH₃ 5.8, NH₂ 2.8, OH 1.4 kJ/mol, same order as the A-values). MMFF94s+ puts F, Cl, Br and C≡N no lower equatorial (Cl −1.5 kJ/mol against an A-value of about +2), so halogens and nitriles get no verdict, and other groups get none until checked. All six dimethylcyclohexanes come out as in the textbook. **Guess first** (off by default) hides the answer until the student picks a chair.
     - **Ring angles:** for each ring, the angles at its atoms measured on the model, compared with the ideal (109.5° sp³, 120° sp²), with a short note by ring size.
   - **Reactions:** the 3D conditions of SN2, SN1/E1, E2 and addition to a C=C, as four principles, on the molecule shown. Which mechanism wins depends on the reagent and solvent, which this tab does not judge; it gives no rates or product ratios. Products are made from the model itself: an atom is changed, removed or added where the mechanism puts it, OpenChemLib reads the new compound's stereo from those coordinates, and the product is built fresh (MMFF94s+) and shown beside the model (one or two models to the right). No coordinates are made up: everything shown is a built structure, or a real one moved rigidly (turned about a single bond, a ring flip from the conformer search). `test.mjs` checks every product against the textbook product as RDKit canonical isomeric SMILES, with RDKit reading the product's own 3D molfile (OpenChemLib's SMILES writer can give conflicting `/` `\` on a C=C carbon with a group on each side).
     - **C–X bond** (principles 1–3): each C–Cl, C–Br and C–I on an sp³ carbon, equivalent ones once (mirror-image ones too). Without one, the page says why: C–F (fluoride leaves poorly), C–OH (needs acid first), a halogen on a C=C or ring carbon (no SN1 or SN2). Rate verdicts follow the textbook rules and are given only when the carbon's other groups are C or H.
     - **Backside attack (SN2):** an OH⁻ arrow coming in 180° from X, and pink spheres on the atoms in its path (within 60° of the line and 3.0 Å of the carbon, in this conformation; `test.mjs`: bromomethane 0 < bromoethane 1 < 2-bromopropane 2 < 2-bromo-2-methylpropane 3, neopentyl bromide 3). Methyl and 1° fast, 2° slow, 3° and neopentyl none (no product shown). The product is the X turned into an O placed on the back side, so it comes out inverted, shown on the reactant turned inside out; R/S before and after, with a note that the letter can stay the same ((R)-1-bromo-1-chloroethane gives (R)-1-chloroethanol). Every product equals the reactant SMILES with Br → O and that center's @/@@ inverted.
     - **Flat intermediate (SN1, E1):** the carbocation, built from the model with X taken off and a + on the carbon (MMFF94s+ handles it; only a converged run counts), drawn when its three bond angles add to 355° or more, with its empty p orbital and water coming to each face; then water on each face gives two alcohols, and the page says how they relate: one product, a pair of enantiomers (racemic, 50:50), or diastereomers (not equal amounts). 3° good, 2° slow, 1° and methyl none, allylic and benzylic by resonance (with a note that water can also add at the far end of an allylic cation). A note when a hydride or methyl shift from a neighbor carbon would make a more substituted cation (2-bromo-3-methylbutane; products from that are not shown), and one that a radical is flat too. MMFF94s+ builds ring 3° carbocations slightly pyramidal (354.5–354.7°), so those are not drawn, with the number given; their two faces still give the products.
     - **Anti-periplanar H and X (E2):** each H on a β carbon. Outside a ring the Cα–Cβ bond turns (rigidly, as on the Shape tab) until H–C–C–X is 180°, seen down the bond; the alkene from that shape comes out E or Z as OpenChemLib reads it from the 3D (the groups that sat gauche end up cis). In a ring the bond cannot turn: E2 needs H and X both axial, which one chair gives when they are trans; the page decides by configuration, says when a ring flip is needed (and shows the other chair when the conformer search builds it), and says when an H is cis to X and can never be anti. The alkenes that can form are listed, the more substituted one marked Zaitsev (usually major with a small base, Hofmann with tert-butoxide), with a note when it cannot form. `test.mjs`: (R)-2-bromobutane's two C3 H give (E)- and (Z)-2-butene and C1 gives 1-butene; meso-1,2-dibromo-1,2-diphenylethane gives only (E)-1-bromo-1,2-diphenylethene and (1R,2R) only (Z); menthyl chloride (all equatorial) gives only 2-menthene, against Zaitsev; neomenthyl chloride (Cl axial) gives 2- and 3-menthene, 3-menthene the Zaitsev one.
     - **Faces of a double bond:** each C=C outside aromatic rings, its p-orbital lobes above and below, and three symmetric additions: H₂/Pd and OsO₄ (syn, both atoms on one face) and Br₂ (anti, Br⁺ as a bromonium on one face, Br⁻ from the other). The two products (first atom on the top face or on the bottom one) are shown as formed, then compared: one product (no new stereocenter), one meso compound, a pair of enantiomers (racemic) or diastereomers. Additions that need a Markovnikov choice (HBr, hydroboration, halohydrins) are left out. `test.mjs`: cis-2-butene + Br₂ → (2R,3R)/(2S,3S)-2,3-dibromobutane, trans → meso; cis + OsO₄ → meso-butane-2,3-diol, trans → the (R,R)/(S,S) pair; cyclohexene + Br₂ → trans-1,2-dibromocyclohexane pair, + OsO₄ → cis-diol (meso); 1,2-dimethylcyclohexene + H₂ → cis-1,2-dimethylcyclohexane (meso); propene + Br₂ → a pair; 2-methylpropene + Br₂ and cis-2-butene + H₂ → one product.
     - **Guess first** (off by default): R/S of the SN2 product, how the SN1 or addition products relate, and which alkene a picked β H gives (or none).
4. **Viewer.** [3Dmol.js](https://3dmol.org).

## Files

- `index.html`: layout and styles
- `app.js`: search, viewer, buttons
- `worker.js`: 3D build, group search, polarity, aromatic rings, equivalent H, Fischer projections, ring flips, mirror planes, comparisons and reaction products (OpenChemLib)
- `chem.js`: functional-group list and the chemistry helpers
- `lookup.js`: name → structure (OPSIN, then PubChem with retry)
- `test.mjs`: self-check (`npm install && npm test`, about 3 minutes): functional groups over ~50 molecules, hybridization, lone pairs, bond angles, polar/nonpolar for the textbook set, π electrons per ring atom and ring system, ¹H NMR signal counts, kit counts, Newman bond lists, their order and steric pairs (cross-checked against the 3D model and the textbook strain increments), ring angles, chairs (which rings count, axial/equatorial against the textbook cis/trans table, 1,3-diaxial pairs against distances, flipped chairs as true MMFF94s+ minima with every group swapped, which chair is more stable for the textbook cases, MMFF94s+ against A-values), hand-checked CIP priorities, E/Z, ring cis/trans, R/S, mirror-image and isomer-relationship checks against [RDKit](https://www.rdkit.org) (a test-only dependency), meso mirror planes checked on the coordinates, Fischer projections against textbook layouts and R/S, and the Reactions tab's products against the textbook ones (SN2 inversion, SN1 faces, E2 alkenes with E/Z and Zaitsev, syn and anti additions), each also on the SMILES that OPSIN or PubChem return for the textbook names

## Notes

- A link like `…/mol3d/#caffeine` opens that molecule directly.
- PubChem allows about 5 requests per second per source. A whole class behind one school IP can hit that together, so `lookup.js` retries a busy reply up to 3 times after a random wait, which spreads the requests out. Names OPSIN can parse never reach PubChem.
- Molecules with large fused ring systems (steroids, taxol) take several seconds to build the first time.
- The 3D build is one conformer, not always the lowest: methylcyclohexane and cis-1,3-dimethylcyclohexane come out with CH₃ axial. Ring flip shows the other chair and says which is more stable. A ring flip takes about a second.
